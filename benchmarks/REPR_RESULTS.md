# AMD64 graph-representation experiments — results

Recorded 2026-11-12 from source revision `b730592`.

- **Machine:** shared cloud VM, 4 virtual CPUs, Intel Xeon (family 6, model
  207: Emerald Rapids) at a nominal 2.10 GHz, Linux 6.18. BMI1/BMI2 and
  AVX-512 F/BW/VL are available and are used only by the kernels that say so.
- **Reference:** kernel `asm`, the unmodified kernel of
  `solvers/level/experimental/amd64/asm/search_sysv.S` (machine code SHA-256
  beginning `813d603827156ce1`, as in `ASM_RESULTS.md`). Every number in the
  first three sections is a speedup relative to it: above 1 is faster.
- **Tool:** `./build/repr_comparison` (method in its header and in
  `README.md` here). Kernel only, no wrapper. Pseudo-random targets, each
  search capped at 200,000 steps, at least 1.5 million steps per pass,
  round-robin over kernels, median of per-round ratios.
- **Ticks** are time-stamp-counter ticks per search step. Hardware counters
  are not available in this VM.
- Every kernel agreed with the reference on the result and step count of
  every search in every run (the tool exits non-zero otherwise).

What each kernel is: see
`solvers/level/experimental/amd64/repr/README.md`.

  GCC build's runs and 76–86% in the Clang build's (the kernels are the same
  machine code in both builds; the two builds were run minutes apart).

  instructions.

  recorded in `ASM_RESULTS.md` (3.29–3.40, 2.67–2.84 there).

## 5. How much of the time is branch misprediction

The same kind of search on the reference kernel (`asm`), measured once with
a workload small enough for the branch predictor to learn by heart (about
20,000 steps, repeated) and once with the standard 1.5 million steps.

| N | L | small workload: steps, ticks per step | standard workload: steps, ticks per step | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 12 | 20,885, 1.45 | 1,500,369, 4.38 | 3.0 |
| 4 | 16 | 21,756, 1.44 | 1,502,284, 4.45 | 3.1 |
| 6 | 12 | 20,396, 1.47 | 1,500,250, 4.59 | 3.1 |
| 6 | 16 | 29,378, 1.35 | 1,516,874, 3.75 | 2.8 |
| 8 | 12 | 20,236, 1.75 | 1,500,042, 4.02 | 2.3 |
| 8 | 16 | 25,331, 1.36 | 1,509,251, 3.66 | 2.7 |

(The `ref` columns of `repr_comparison -k b4 -n 4,6,8 -l 12,16 -s 20000`
and of the same command without `-s`.)

With its branches predicted, the kernel needs 1.4–1.8 ticks per step; in
realistic use it needs 3.7–4.6. Between 56% and 68% of the time of a realistic
search is therefore the cost of branches the processor cannot predict, and
no way of storing the graph touches that directly. This is a controlled
experiment, not a counter reading, but it is the measurement behind the
analysis in the README.

It also explains two things seen above: why the gains from better
representations are tens of percent and not multiples, and why the very
small workloads of section 4 behave differently from the rest.
