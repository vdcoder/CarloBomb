# 27 and 28 bits by shared search: EXPERIMENTAL, NOT CERTIFIED

The Captain and the CTO approved running the shared-search experiment in
full at 27 and 28 bits in the development container. These are the
results.

**Nothing on this page is a certified result.** No Bomb of 27 or 28 bits
had been certified when these runs were made; the Captain's construction of
Bomb(27) was running, on another machine, and was not touched. What stands
here is what the experiment found, checked as far as an experiment can
check itself, and written down so that the certified Bombs can be held
against it the moment they exist. `FULL_RUNS.md` says what agreement of
this kind does and does not mean.

Run on 15 November 2026 in the development container (4 virtual CPUs, Intel
Xeon under KVM, 15.7 GB, GCC 13.3 `-O3`), 4 processes at low priority beside
other work, 512 classes. Each level was built as the ladder builds a level:
on the table one bit shorter, each string begun at what its first bits
need. The 27-bit level stands on the 26-bit table of `FULL_RUNS.md`, whose
checksum is that of the certified Bomb(26); the 28-bit level stands on the
experimental 27-bit table. The logs are in `runs/` (`c27.txt`,
`after27.txt`, `c28.txt`, `after28.txt`, and `n27.txt` for the run from
nothing at the end of this page).

## What was checked

For each of the two tables:

1. **Every REV on the reference interpreter**, by the experiment, after
   the search: a REV of the nodes recorded, emitting its string, visiting
   every one of its nodes, its complement emitting the complement.
   67,108,864 of 67,108,864 at 27 bits, 134,217,728 of 134,217,728 at 28 bits.
2. **By the library's writer**, which runs every REV again before it
   writes an entry.
3. **By the production verifier**, `carlobomb bomb verify`, on the file:

   | Check | 27 bits | 28 bits |
   |---|---|---|
   | header, length, checksums, counts and flags | pass | pass |
   | every entry decoded and its REV run | pass, 134,217,728 REVs | pass, 268,435,456 REVs |
   | complement symmetry of REVs and node counts | pass | pass |
   | against the table one bit shorter: the minimum of every shorter string is the smallest among its extensions, and no stretch needs over one node more than the string | pass, against the 26-bit table | pass, against the experimental 27-bit table |
   | strings drawn at random and solved again from one node up by the production solver (`--recertify --sample 1000`) | 1,000 drawn, 1,000 reproduced | 1,000 drawn, 1,000 reproduced |
   | the strings of the largest minimum solved again (`--recertify --hardest`) | all 8 strings of 10 nodes, reproduced | 100 of the 166 strings of 10 nodes, reproduced |

4. **The node-count digest two ways**: from the search, and by
   `nodes_digest` reading the compact file made of the table.

As in `FULL_RUNS.md`: the REVs prove that every string needs at most the
nodes stated. That it needs no fewer is, for these two lengths, a claim of
the experiment and of nobody else yet. The files written say "certified
minimum" of every entry, because only then can their bytes be compared
with a certified file's; they were written to the scratch directory of the
container, named `experimental-27.frb` and `experimental-28.frb`, in no
repository and in no place a Bomb is taken from, and the container does
not keep them. What is kept of them is this page and the logs.

the 28-bit table has the 27-bit one under it, which this run supports. (The
log of this run ends without the line for its exit code: the script that
writes it had been stopped so that the 28-bit run would not start. The run
itself went to its end, as its last lines show.)
